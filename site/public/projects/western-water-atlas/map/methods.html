Western Water Atlas · Oct 13, 2026 publication snapshot

How to read the water

Start with the place and the date. Water in a reservoir, water passing a gauge, legal entitlements and water consumed during a year answer different questions. An acre-foot is the volume covering one acre one foot deep; the atlas keeps volumes in acre-feet and snowpack in inches of water.

Latest conditions means the newest qualified record in this acquired snapshot, not a live operations feed. Each agency’s annual report keeps its own year. Geometry is reference geography, not today’s shoreline.

When a difference is meaningful

The comparison resolver requires matching recipient, accounting boundary, quantity type, definition, unit, frequency and the exact start/end/calendar convention of the period. Both values must be finite. A mismatch stays useful side-by-side context but has no computed difference. Availability minus consumption is a source-defined accounting comparison, not a determination of compliance.

Explore without losing your place

Ordinary map clicks and related-feature buttons keep the camera fixed. Explore searches all named entries and aliases; its result click deliberately navigates. Layers change what is drawn and picked. Trace itself preserves the camera; Fit is explicit; Exit restores the original camera and layers. Methods, Data and About use same-origin navigation and the map restores its session state on return. No arbitrary return URL is accepted. If session storage is denied, this convenience is unavailable but the map works.

Where the water goes · M5 review

Census 2020 population controls label eligibility: at wide zoom at least 250,000, mid zoom 25,000, close zoom 10,000. Corrected Census 2020 urban areas are subtly shaded and labeled once; small places inside an urban area do not get another dot-and-name. These footprints are urban geography, not utility boundaries. GEOIDs preserve namesake identity. Community context cites operators and distinguishes groundwater replenishment from direct delivery.

Crop value estimates use the same crop, state and harvest year: statewide NASS gross production value divided by harvested acres, then by the 2023 Irrigation and Water Management Survey’s mean acre-feet applied per irrigated acre. Annual crop statistics and irrigation survey populations differ. This is a state-scale estimate, not a matched-farm measurement, profit, marginal value of water or consumptive use. Rice, potatoes and alfalfa hay are shown; suppressed cotton values remain omitted. Alfalfa is a qualified closest pairing: value and harvested acres cover dry hay and mixtures, while the water category also includes greenchop and silage. Each estimate labels this mismatch.

Delivery prices describe different services: IID canal delivery and MWD untreated or treated wholesale supply. Separate fixed charges can apply. They are context, not a farm-versus-city value ranking.

City water figures retain their own supplier geography, definitions and years. Metro GDP is BEA’s current-dollar, all-industry total, shown through FRED’s public mirror. The last official BEA metropolitan GDP year is 2023; BEA discontinued MSA estimates in February 2026. County GDP is not substituted. Metro GDP is never divided by water.

Powell contour playback

Playback uses daily Reclamation HDB elevation readings (NGVD29) and three published USGS Byerley 2024 outlines at 3,490, 3,520.46 and 3,525 feet NGVD29. It chooses the greatest available contour at or below each day’s elevation. These are coarse operational-tier shapes, not the full five-foot dataset or the actual shoreline. Missing readings remain gaps. Shapes are generalized without interpolation, scaling or morphing. Date, observed elevation and chosen contour stay visible. Playback jumps between real days when the chosen outline changes so the map visibly animates; the slider still exposes every daily position. Satellite polygons remain separately dated check frames. USGS extent polygons.

M6 reference outlines and exploration

All 16 reservoirs have USGS NHD high-resolution waterbody polygons, simplified without scaling. Their label anchors lie inside the reference polygon. These are source-vintage waterbodies, not current shorelines or operational full-pool contours. San Luis is offstream pumped storage; the other 15 have an intersecting source-river geometry. NHD source segments join the Gunnison and Colorado at Grand Junction, without an invented connecting line.

Reservoir polygons accept taps within 16 CSS pixels on desktop and 22 on touch. Reservoir labels have an additional 6-pixel hit buffer. Rivers and canals use 16 pixels on desktop, 20 on touch; points and reservoirs precede nearest-line resolution. Ordinary taps preserve the camera.

Layers → Elevation review options offers the existing paper fade, persistent hillshade and stronger contrast on the same retained relief raster. These are visual explorations, not new elevation data or a final design choice.
