Methods

Definitions, aggregation, route assignment and worked reconciliations.

How to read the map

Line width represents annual throughput at that location in the selected unit: short tons or dollars. Wider lines carry more, but minimum widths, joins and caps make the numeric card more precise than a pixel comparison. A short ton is 2,000 pounds; dollar figures retain their source year and measure shipment, trade or production value rather than GDP. Zooming reveals finer geometry and additional labels without changing the underlying annual quantities. Click a line to inspect its local interval, or a place to see its reporting geography, quantities and available direction or partner detail. Follow the calculation link for the definition, and use Data to check the source vintage and coverage.

Measurement conventions

The nominal edition is a 2025 snapshot, not a single-year census. Source years span 2022–2025. A short ton is 907.18474 kg. Values are nominal current USD of their source year; benchmark-price estimates are identified separately, and no inflation adjustment is applied.

T denotes short tons, U current USD and q the selected quantity. o and d index domestic origin and destination, z a FAF zone, e a network edge and τ trade type. Hats denote atlas estimates, Σ a sum, and 1[condition] an indicator equal to one when the condition holds and zero otherwise. Additional indices and symbols are defined in the section where they are used; local meanings are not carried between metric families.

Shipment, passage, handling, customs trade and production are distinct measures. Source-estimated quantities and additional atlas assignment or valuation are identified with the calculation. See Limitations for missing data, overlap and uncertainty.

Definitions and aggregation

qodmcτ is shipment quantity indexed by domestic mode m and SCTG2 commodity c, in addition to the origin, destination and trade indices defined above. FAF trade types 1, 2 and 3 denote domestic, imports and exports. The preliminary 2024 columns are converted before aggregation: T = 1,000 × tons_2024 and U = 1,000,000 × current_value_2024.

Oz = Σd,m,c,τ qz d m c τ
Wz = Σm,c,τ qz z m c τ
Sz = Oz − Wz
Rz,K = Σo≠z,m∈K,c,τ qo z m c τ, K = {1, 2, 3, 6}

O is originating freight, including within-region W; S is freight shipped outside. R is received from outside in the four retained detail modes: truck, rail, water and pipeline. The delivered zoneOut and zoneWithin arrays contain all-mode domestic/import/export tons followed by those three dollar totals. Runtime regional o and w retain these marginals. Retained od[m] rows are [origin, destination, trade type, tons, dollars, energy-class tons…]; they do not contain an all-commodity joint distribution.

For each retained mode, outside inbound and outbound exclude o = d; within includes only o = d. Partners group outside rows by the other endpoint and rank their inbound + outbound quantity in the selected unit. The leading partners plus all remaining partners reconcile to the retained four-mode exchanged quantity. Other-mode shipped/within remainders equal the respective all-mode totals minus the retained subtotals, subject to rounding. All-mode inbound and its remainder are not calculated by the served card.

Geography and rounding

FAF regions are freight statistical areas, including separately named parts of metropolitan areas and rest-of-state regions. A city label does not replace the FAF boundary or join multistate parts. The extractor sums original records in floating point, rounds OD cells to 0.1 unit and regional/national marginal cells to whole units. Independently rounded marginals therefore need not equal sums of rounded OD cells exactly.

Separate retained mode|all source tables cover all eight modes. Their internal reconstruction reconciles originating and within quantities for 132 regions (maximum absolute discrepancy 1.22 short tons; exact dollars in the tested marginals). This is source-only evidence: it does not extend the four-mode served received, partner or mode detail. Air, multiple modes/mail, other and unknown modes must not be synthesized from marginal totals.

County weighting and demand disaggregation

County interior points are assigned to the smallest containing FAF polygon; if none contains the point, the closest same-state polygon is used. Gazetteer points outside the largest county land polygon are replaced by that polygon’s representative point. Virginia BEA county/city combinations are attached to the county point. BEA 2024 CAGDP2 origin weights use line 91 (private goods-producing GDP) + line 34 (wholesale trade); destination weights use line 1 (all-industry GDP). GDP provides a spatial allocation weight, not a conversion from value added to shipment dollars.

For the equations below, c indexes counties, s states and ℓ GDP industry lines. g is county GDP and G its state total; a and b are normalized origin and destination shares for clusters i and j. P is an assigned path, κ its edge cost and L accumulated link quantity. Demand qodτ sums the mode’s commodities.

ĝcℓ = max(0, Gsℓ − Σdisclosed c′∈s gc′ℓ) × gc1 / Σsuppressed c′∈s gc′1
azi = Σc∈cluster i(gc91 + gc34) / Σc∈z(gc91 + gc34)
bzj = Σc∈cluster j gc1 / Σc∈z gc1
qoi,dj,τ = qodτ aoi bdj

The first equation imputes a suppressed line-91 or line-34 weight from the state’s undisclosed remainder, in proportion to disclosed all-industry GDP. The imputation affects routing only. Counties rank by normalized origin + destination weights. Greedy spatial seeds are separated by 0.25 degree-equivalent distance in metro regions (maximum four points) and 0.55 in rest/state regions (maximum 12, or 15 when county count exceeds 100). Distance uses longitude scaled by cosine of mean latitude. Remaining counties join their closest seed. A zone without counties receives one internal point with shares 1 and 1. Exported shares round to six decimal places: conservation is exact before share rounding, approximate afterward; zero-denominator weights are not observed shipment absence.

Path assignment and link accumulation

Poi,dj = arg minP Σe∈P κe
Leτ = Σo≠d,i,j qodτ aoi bdj 1[e∈Poi,dj]

Dijkstra produces a least-cost tree per origin point. Destination demands accumulate back along tree arcs through a sparse triangular solve. Virtual source nodes have outgoing access arcs only; virtual sink nodes have incoming arcs only, preventing transit through an allocation point. Each point connects to up to three network nodes, rejecting picks within 0.04 degree of an earlier pick. The nearby search radius is a preference: the producer permits a first pick beyond it. Access miles are 69.05 × √((Δlongitude cos latitude)² + Δlatitude²).

Truck edge cost is FHWA LENGTH × highway-class multiplier: 0.85 for classes 11/21, 0.92 for 12, 0.95 for 22, 1.25 for 19 and 1 otherwise. Class 41 and AK/HI highway geometry are excluded. Paired divided carriageways are collapsed to one retained line using proximity and opposite bearing tests; links are treated as two-way. Access candidates lie in the largest strongly connected core and on selected major classes; radius preference 0.25 degree and access cost 1.4 × miles + 0.5. Costs are generalized miles, not travel time.

Intrazonal demand is excluded from interregional routing but remains in regional totals. Alaska/Hawaii FAF regions 020, 151 and 159 have no lower-48 network assignment. Remote or disconnected demand is reported separately, not assigned a measured zero flow. Output stores [T domestic, T imports, T exports, U domestic, U imports, U exports] per source edge; the card uses its clicked local source interval. A named route, blended drawing chain and local edge need not carry the same quantity.

Main-line assignment

Rail uses the county weights, point disaggregation, least-cost paths and link accumulation defined in Roads. Only the network and access costs differ.

Rail uses BTS NTAD North American Rail Network records with NET = M, valid FRFRANODE/TOFRANODE, and distinct endpoints. Cost is MILES (minimum 0.0001), with arcs in both directions. 2025 lock scaling and valuation

For river system r, commodity class c and year y, Arc,y is the sum of LPMS annual commodity tons across locks that report both 2023 and 2025. Missing year pairs are excluded from both sums. The river-system ratio is used only if Arc,2023 ≥ 500,000 and Arc,2025 > 0. Otherwise the national paired-lock class ratio is used. In the executable producer, a national class with a zero baseline receives factor 1; a class absent from the national dictionary uses the overall national paired-lock ratio.

f is the selected scaling factor, p the FAF commodity USD/ton rate, and r(e) the link’s river system; ↑/↓ denote upbound/downbound.

frc = Arc,2025 / Arc,2023 (eligible river/class); otherwise national fallback
T̂e,c,↑/↓ = Te,c,↑/↓,2023 fr(e),c
pc = UFAF water,c,2024 / TFAF water,c,2024

FAF’s WCSC approximation maps SCTG15→coal; 16–19→petroleum; 20–24→chemicals; 10–14,25–29,31→crude materials; 1–9→farm; 32–33→manufactures; 34–38→machinery; 41→waste; all remaining SCTG codes→manufactures. The retained summary reports national overall paired-lock factor 0.9890591223, but the retained link-weighted scaling factor is 1.0059696108: river/class composition differs. These are not contradictory national shipment estimates. The derivative contains 1,575 geometry records; multipart source links can repeat a link ID and value. Neither geometry-record count nor sum of repeated link tons is unique cargo.

EIA controls and calibration

EIA monthly 2024/2025 series are summed by calendar year. Cross-PADD crude receipts PET.MCRMPP{dest}P{origin}1.M are converted from thousand barrels by ×1,000 ×0.1504 assumed short tons/barrel. Products use max(0, total petroleum MTTMPP − crude MCRMPP) ×1,000 ×0.1396. These density assumptions apply to pipeline calibration, not production-card native volumes.

a and b index origin/destination PADDs; E is the EIA petroleum receipt control after mass conversion, F the corresponding FAF class tons, and f the selected calibration factor.

fab,k = min(2, max(0.5, Eab,k,2025 / Fab,k,2024)) when E > 0
T̂od,k = fod,kTod,k; Ûod,k = fod,kUod,k

F sums FAF class tons for a directed PADD pair. Zero/absent EIA receipt controls use the national growth ratio, not a zero factor. Intra-PADD and other pairs use national ratios: crude field production PET.MCRFPUS1.M, products inter-PADD receipts and marketed gas NG.N9050US2.M. Retained ratios are respectively 1.0269681267, 1.0441821597 and 1.0445878303. Gas/HGL uses marketed-gas growth for every pair. Executable constants and retained summaries clip to [0.5,2], using the actual executable bounds.

Graph and incremental assignment

Each class graph nodes geometric line intersections; gas and HGL share one inherited graph. Coordinates round to four decimals for node identity. Dangling endpoints within 0.05 degree of another line receive expensive undrawn connectors to a nearby line endpoint. FAF zones receive an anchor inside or within 0.75 degree of their polygon, searched within 3 degrees of the internal point. Virtual source/sink access connects up to 60 nearby nodes within 1.2 degrees and polygon distance <0.3 degree, or the single anchor fallback; access cost is 0.8 × approximate straight miles + 1.

h indexes assignment increments, α their demand shares, L accumulated tons, C the nominal class cost capacity, and ktype the line-type cost multiplier. κ is generalized edge cost; each P is its least-cost path.

κe,h = milese ktype[1 + 0.15(Le,h/Ck)⁴]
ΔLeτ,h = αh Σod T̂odτ1[e∈Pod,h]
α = [0.25, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.05]

At each increment, Dijkstra assigns demand using costs based on accumulated tonnage. Type multipliers are Interstate 1, Intrastate 1.3, Gathering 3, otherwise 1. Nominal cost capacities C are 40 million crude, 30 million products and 40 million gas/HGL short tons/year; they are parameters, not measured capacities. Dollars accumulate along the same paths. Intrazonal, unanchored and disconnected demands are not drawn; connectors do not acquire observed flow records.

T-100 handling

BTS T-100 domestic/international nonstop segment records retain all twelve months of 2025 and all carriers. Each segment’s short tons equal (FREIGHT + MAIL pounds)/2,000. Domestic records add tons to origin outbound and destination inbound. International records with exactly a U.S. endpoint contribute to that endpoint’s direction; U.S.–U.S. records in the international table are skipped during airport accumulation.

D and I are domestic and international directional handling tons; t is the stored direction vector (zero-based indices).

t = [Dout, Din, Iin, Iout]
Received = t[1] + t[2]; Shipped = t[0] + t[3]
Handled = Σi=0…3 t[i]

The producer rounds each airport direction to whole short tons, then cards sum the vector. Domestic handling counts a domestic segment at both airports; the national domestic segment total counts it once. Neither is a unique shipment or aircraft count.

International customs-dollar allocation

Census air imports/exports are customs-port observations. A port explicitly named AIRPORT maps to the busiest international airport within 30 km; other ports consider international airports within 100 km. Retained courier exceptions add MEM to New Orleans 2002 and SDF/CVG/IND to Cleveland 4101. Distances use a 6,371-km spherical great-circle calculation. Ports lacking coordinates or candidates remain unallocated.

a indexes the airport, p the customs port and b other candidate airports. s is an allocation share and Up,direction reported customs-port value.

sap,imports = Ia,in / Σb∈candidates(p)Ib,in
sap,exports = Ia,out / Σb∈candidates(p)Ib,out
Ûa,direction = round[Σp Up,directionsap,direction]

A direction with no T-100 tons splits equally across candidates. The runtime dollar vector u = [imports, exports] contains allocated international dollars only; domestic dollars are unavailable, not zero. Even a single-airport assignment remains a geographic allocation. uEst flags multiple-airport splits, not every modeled assignment.

Airport partners rank two-way T-100 tons, including in the dollar view; dollar partner rankings cannot be inferred. T-100 country partners and Census country-weight allocations are distinct fields. The latter apply the same port shares to customs air weight, converting kg to short tons. The retained sample filters U.S. airports to ≥50,000 handling tons or ≥$1 billion allocated value, then takes the union of top 28 by tons and top 20 by dollars (30 runtime airports in the audited payload), not every airport.

Independent source universes

National totals derive from source records rather than summing visible layers or selected marker samples. For FAF trade type τ, national[τ|m] contains independently rounded 2024 short tons and current dollars aggregated across original SCTG/OD records. Census 2025 totals use normalized annual customs records. T-100 segment handling is a third universe.

qFAF,τ = Σall modes m national[τ|m]
TCensus,d = (Wvessel,d + Wair,d) / 907.18474
UCensus,d = Uall modes,d
Air mass share = Wair / (Wair + Wvessel)
Air value share = Uair / Uall modes

Weights W are kg, values U current USD, and d imports or exports. Land-mode weights are not published in this retained Census weight universe; the mass-share denominator is therefore vessel+air, not all freight. The value-share denominator includes all trade modes. National domestic T-100 segment tons equal Σ(FREIGHT+MAIL)/2,000 with each domestic segment counted once, unlike the sum of airport inbound+outbound handling.

Layer visibility does not alter these statistical universes. Source accounting was checked against the frozen core payload for each FAF trade type/unit, Census import/export weight/value and both air-share denominators.

Quantitative encodings and local facts

Rendering changes geometry/detail, not source-year quantities. In the inspected runtime, λ is the zoom-transform scale (runtime k), λ₀ its fitted home-view scale (runtime KH), and σ the projection scale. WPER = 0.085 × max(0.6, min(1.1, σ/1,500)). Quantity is normalized linearly; the exponent 0.32 applies to zoom only, not tonnage.

qdisplay = T/10⁶ (tons view), or U/(3 × 10⁹) (dollars view)
wpx = min(17, max(0.12, qdisplay × WPER × (λ/λ₀)0.32))

The width function is a display transform, not a mass conversion: $3 billion and 1 million tons have the same baseline width by design. Ribbon handling, joined strokes, minimum widths and caps prevent direct pixel-ratio interpretation. A card reports the clicked source interval, not a blended display-chain average or whole named-route total. Network labels can use visible/named-group peaks and rounded quantities, rather than the clicked edge’s value.

In the inspected baseline, port circle size remains based on vessel tons even in dollar mode. Airport diamonds switch between handling tons and allocated international dollars with minimum/maximum sizes. Metro dots are label anchors, not quantitative circles; label priority uses domestic + export originating tons, excluding the import leg. Duplicate display names can collapse labels. Producing-area outlines describe geographic context, not quantity-proportional area; raw-material admission can use dollar thresholds even in the tons view. Native-energy tons placeholders are not measured mass.

Continuous-zoom admission, source/detail dependencies, simplification, priority and label collision rules determine visibility. Missing labels or omitted lines are not evidence of absent freight. Insets preserve source scope but use separate cartographic framing. Loading progress is preparation state; animated vehicles are not live tracked cargo. Display admission is not a national tonnage cutoff.

Limitations

The retained production inventory contains 189 records, not a complete census of U.S. production. Selected gateways, network exclusions and suppressed source cells limit coverage. Missing, withheld, unallocated and unassigned quantities are unknown, not observed zero; compact zero placeholders do not override source scope.

  • Measures overlap. A shipment can appear on successive network links, at a gateway and in regional trade. Parent and child production areas can overlap. These quantities cannot be added into a national physical-economy total. Gross shipment and production values are not GDP, value added or profit.
  • Assignment is modeled. Truck and rail links are not traffic counts or waybills. Pipeline geometry does not validate local throughput, gas/HGL compatibility, operator dispatch or an exact shipment route. Water lock ratios proxy change on links; they are not 2025 link meters.
  • Geography has different roles. FAF regions, customs reporting ports, production regions and contextual outlines do not necessarily coincide. Statewide mineral values are not local deposit values. County memberships and generalized boundaries are not certified production footprints.
  • Some provenance is incomplete. Original waterway tonnage input rows were not recovered for full replay. The STEO extraction’s release/acquisition lineage and some geometry update dates remain unverified. Other acquisition dates are unknown unless recorded. Data identifies these gaps by dataset.
  • Reconciliation is not an error bound. The retained tests reproduce arithmetic and accounting. They do not establish sampling-error intervals, route-validation accuracy or local allocation uncertainty. No unsupported confidence intervals are assigned.
