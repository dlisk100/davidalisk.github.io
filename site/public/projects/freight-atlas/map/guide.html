How to read the atlas

A quick reference for the map’s marks and measures.

This quick reference remains available for existing links. The main reading guide is now at the top of Methods.

Start with a place

  1. Scroll or pinch to explore. More geometry and labels appear as you zoom.
  2. Switch between tons and dollars. One short ton is 2,000 pounds; dollars retain their source year.
  3. Click a visible place or line to inspect its quantity and reporting scope.
  4. Follow the calculation link for its definition and Data for source vintages.

Read the marks

Lines
Width encodes local annual throughput or value. Minimum widths, joins and caps mean the numeric card is more precise than a pixel comparison.
Ports and airports
Ports show international vessel customs trade. Airports show freight-plus-mail handling; airport dollars are allocated international customs value.
Freight regions
Labels refer to FAF statistical regions, not municipal limits. Some metros have separately reported parts; other regions cover the rest of a state.
Producing areas
Outlines provide geographic context, not quantity-proportional area. Native barrels, cubic feet and bushels are distinct from freight short tons.
Labels and motion
Labels are selected for readability. Animated illustrations are not tracked shipments.

Compare like measures

Network throughput, gateway handling, customs trade and production can describe the same goods at different stages. Do not add them together, sum successive links as unique cargo, or combine parent and child production values.

The 2025 edition includes actual observations from different years. Source-year dollars have not been inflation-adjusted. Missing, withheld and unassigned data are not observed zero.
